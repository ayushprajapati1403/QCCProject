## Synthesis: the unresolved weakness of QPSO in discrete cloud scheduling

QPSO's only search operator is x = p ± α·|mbest − x|·ln(1/u): a Laplace-distributed jump whose centre p is a random convex combination of pbest and gbest and whose scale is the particle's distance to the swarm's mean-best (Sun et al. 2011, Eqs. 7–13; Mikki & Kishk 2006). Three properties collide with task-to-VM assignment.

1. Attractor collapse. Every coordinate converges to p inside the hyper-rectangle spanned by pbest and gbest; once gbest sits in a local optimum "the particle's current position … will also be dragged into that point, leading the algorithm to premature convergence" (AMC 2011). Remedies (Cauchy/Lévy mutation, Gaussian attractors, rank-weighted mbest, annealed α) leave the structural fact that the step scale |mbest − x| shrinks to zero as the swarm agrees.
2. Lossy discretisation. Cloud papers either round the continuous coordinate to a resource index (Yu 2021; Wang et al. integer-PSO sibling; Aminu 2026) or drop the delta well for Q-bit registers decoded by hashing (Naik/Bey; Balicki 2022) — an EDA, not QPSO. Under rounding the map from Laplace jumps to assignments is many-to-one: sub-unit jumps are absorbed, large jumps become near-uniform random reassignments; the spread-scaled exploration logic has no defined meaning on an assignment lattice. Wang et al. (2008) noted QPSO's combinatorial use was "rather limited"; Li, Fang & Zhu (2023, 2024) still report "discretization issues" and "aggregation among particles".
3. Static-batch evaluation. Every verified QPSO-proper cloud study optimises a fixed batch; none re-optimises under arrivals or VM churn.

## Answers
(a) The delta-well density is Q(x) = (1/L)exp(−2|x − p|/L); inverting its CDF gives |x − p| = (L/2)ln(1/u): the Laplace quantile. Stated by Mikki & Kishk 2006 ("QDPSO … has a Laplace distribution"), Sun et al. 2011 ("double exponential distribution"), Sun et al. 2012 EVCO (bare-bones PSO family), Li et al. 2023.
(b) Most frequently stated weakness: premature convergence from attractor/mbest collapse (Sun 2011; Li 2014; Yuan 2019; Liu 2022; He & Lu 2026); for discrete problems: "discretization issues" and "aggregation among particles" (Li et al. 2023/2024), "rather limited" combinatorial use (Wang 2008).
(c) No verified QPSO-proper cloud paper tests dynamic arrivals or VM churn (Yu 2021; Balicki 2022; Elsedimy 2025; Naik 2025 are static). Claims of dynamics without an online protocol: Aminu 2026; Lilhore 2025 (QIEA), Mishra 2021 (salp), Gurrala 2026.
(d) Code: MEALPY 3.1.0 `PSO.QDPSO`; github.com/ngroup/qpso; github.com/sznczy/quantum-particle-swarm-optimization; github.com/ShivayaDevs/QPSO_RCPSP. None of the cloud QPSO papers releases code.
